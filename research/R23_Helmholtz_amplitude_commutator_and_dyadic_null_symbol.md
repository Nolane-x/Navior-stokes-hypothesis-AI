# R23 — Helmholtz amplitude commutator and dyadic null symbol

**Status:** `exact harmonic-analysis representation / not claimed novel`  
**Depends on:** R02–R03, R21–R22  
**Clay status:** exposes a cancellation but does not provide the missing a-priori critical bound

R22 rewrites the nonlinear L3 test field as

`Q(rho u)=Q(A×grad rho)`, `rho=|u|`, `curl A=u`.

RD012 proves that the raw vector-potential product may be arbitrarily larger than its gradient projection. R23 identifies the exact cancellation responsible for that difference.

## 1. Exact commutator identity

For a divergence-free periodic velocity,

> `Q u=0`.

Let `M_rho` denote multiplication by `rho`. Then

> `Q(rho u)`
>
> `= Q M_rho u - M_rho Q u`
>
> `= [Q,M_rho]u`.

Thus the critical L3 pressure-test defect is exactly a commutator between the gradient Helmholtz projector and multiplication by the speed.

In particular, a spatially constant speed multiplier commutes on divergence-free fields and gives no defect. Only spatial variation of `rho` can generate the pressure test field, which is the bulk harmonic-analysis version of the R09 quotient-by-speed observation.

## 2. Exact Fourier triad symbol

Use Fourier convention `exp(i k·x)`. For an interaction

`p+q=k`,

with `p·uhat(p)=0`, the direct product contributes

`rhohat(q) uhat(p)`

to `(rho u)^hat(k)`. The gradient projection at nonzero `k` is

`Q_k f = k [k·f]/|k|^2`.

Since

`k·uhat(p)=(p+q)·uhat(p)=q·uhat(p)`,

we obtain the exact null-form formula

> `widehat{Q(rho u)}(k)`
>
> `= [k/|k|^2] sum_{p+q=k} [uhat(p)·q] rhohat(q)`.

This is the same cancellation seen from R22's vector-potential form. The apparent high derivative in `A×grad rho` does not survive as `|q|/|p|` growth in every paraproduct: incompressibility moves the load into the commutator symbol.

## 3. High-velocity / low-amplitude-frequency gain

For a single interaction,

`|Q_k[rhohat(q)uhat(p)]|`

`<= [|q|/|k|] |uhat(p)| |rhohat(q)|`.

If

`|p| >= 4 |q|`,

then

`|k|=|p+q| >= |p|-|q| >= (3/4)|p|`,

and therefore

> `|Q_k[rhohat(q)uhat(p)]|`
>
> `<= (4/3) [|q|/|p|] |uhat(p)| |rhohat(q)|`.

Hence the paraproduct in which the **velocity frequency is much higher than the amplitude frequency** carries a genuine low/high ratio gain.

This is a useful one-sided null structure. The opposite low-velocity/high-amplitude interaction has no comparable gain from this identity alone, and high-high interactions remain potentially dangerous.

## 4. Relation to classical Calderon commutators

The matrix Helmholtz projector is a zero-order Calderon–Zygmund/Riesz-transform operator (up to the usual sign convention, its entries are products of Riesz transforms). Thus R23 places the pressure-test field directly in the classical commutator framework.

The Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss theory and later Riesz-transform commutator work show that commutators with Calderon–Zygmund operators are controlled on `L^p`, `1<p<infinity`, by the BMO size of the multiplier. On the torus, the periodic analogue gives schematically

> `||Q(rho u)||_p <= C_p ||rho||_BMO ||u||_p`.

This is a **literature interface**, not a newly proved estimate in this repository and not a closure theorem. The important point is that the correct harmonic-analysis object is a commutator, not a generic product.

Primary/modern source interface:

- I. Holmes, M. T. Lacey, B. D. Wick, *Commutators in the Two-Weight Setting*, arXiv:1506.05747, which recalls and extends the classical Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss Riesz-commutator theory.

## 5. Why the known commutator theorem does not solve the PDE

At `p=2`, the schematic BMO estimate would give

`||Q(rho u)||_2 <= C ||rho||_BMO ||u||_2`.

Energy controls `||u||_2`, but it does not provide the required critical-in-time control of `||rho||_BMO`. The critical Sobolev embedding `W^{1,3}->BMO` only moves the missing estimate to a scale-critical `L^3` amplitude-gradient quantity, which is not furnished by the energy inequality.

Likewise, R03 pairs this test field with `Q(omega×u)`, whose critical high-frequency action is exactly part of the unresolved R18–R20 frontier.

Thus the classical commutator theorem is compatible with the program but does not close it.

## 6. Correct dyadic frontier

R23 suggests separating the projected pressure-test field into paraproduct regimes:

1. **high-u / low-rho:** gains a low/high frequency ratio and is a candidate for summable control;
2. **low-u / high-rho:** no symbol gain from R23; must exploit how high-frequency amplitude is generated by `u` itself;
3. **high-high -> high:** potentially critical and must be coupled to dissipation/Lamb transfer;
4. **high-high -> low:** output is low frequency and may be controlled through finite-frequency energy mechanisms, but the near-cancellation geometry must be audited explicitly.

A decisive next theorem would prove that the first and fourth regimes are harmless and reduce the gradient branch to a sharply localized high-amplitude-frequency interaction whose time action can be compared with R20/C01/C02.

R23 does not yet prove that summability.

## 7. Verification

`verification/check_R23_commutator_null_symbol.py` reconstructs the Fourier symbol with exact rational-complex arithmetic for divergence-free modes, exhaustively checks the high-u/low-rho gain on integer triads satisfying the separation hypothesis, and verifies that a constant amplitude mode contributes zero.
